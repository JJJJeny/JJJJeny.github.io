Contract review with human judgment built in

My teammate and I built a bounded agent to help small businesses review vendor service agreements without hiding the evidence, uncertainty, or final decision from the user.

Product design, workflow, and evaluation
User
Small-business contract reviewer
Control
Human approval for consequential decisions
ProblemAgent designControlsEvaluationLearning

01 · Problem

Small teams still need a first pass before legal review

Vendor agreements repeat many of the same questions: payment terms, renewal, termination, liability, data handling, and service commitments. Small businesses may not have dedicated legal operations to review each document consistently.

We focused on the first-pass workflow. The agent identifies clauses that deserve attention and shows the evidence behind each finding; it does not make the final legal decision.

02 · Agent design

Bound the task before adding autonomy

The workflow breaks contract review into explicit steps: ingest the agreement, extract relevant clauses, compare them with the review criteria, surface risks with supporting text, and route the result to a person.

  • Structured clause extraction
  • Policy and requirement checks
  • Risk findings linked to source evidence
  • Human review before acceptance

Making the stages visible helped us reason about where the system could fail and where a user needed control.

03 · Product controls

Trust comes from inspectability, not confident language

We designed the system to keep supporting evidence close to each finding and to avoid treating an agent recommendation as a final answer. Uncertain or consequential findings remain visible for human review.

Product principle: automate the repetitive reading and routing work while preserving human judgment for contract decisions.

04 · Evaluation

Measure missed risks and false alarms separately

We evaluated the workflow against sample agreements to see whether it found expected issues, cited the correct clause, and routed the result appropriately. False positives matter because a system that flags everything recreates the same review burden it is meant to reduce.

The next evaluation cycle focuses on improving precision while preserving coverage of high-risk clauses.

05 · What I learned

Agentic products need product boundaries as much as model capability

The most important design decision was not which prompt to use. It was defining what the agent could decide, what evidence it had to show, and when it had to stop for a person. Those boundaries made the workflow easier to evaluate and more credible to users.
